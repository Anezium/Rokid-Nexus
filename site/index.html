Roadmap

No dates and no marketing. What is done, what is being written now, what is committed, and the problem each one actually solves — including the ones that turned out to be harder than they looked.

Shipped

Running on hardware, in a public release.

  • 1.4.11

    A notice answers the question you saw

    Replies, gestures and dismissals are bound to the notice and the question on screen, so a late tap can never act on its replacement. A notice keeps the keys over whatever is underneath, and a reply the link rejected says Delivery not confirmed instead of risking a second send.

    Under the hood

    Notice protocol v5 needs both hubs on 1.4.11; plugins rebuilt on SDK 0.19.0 also filter stale callbacks already queued. The phone hub now hands notices, pins, activities, surfaces and Ink to separate routers, and an SPP drop no longer silences a live notice's callbacks.

  • 1.4.10

    Listening with the display asleep

    A plugin holding the microphone can let its surface go and keep listening. The display sleeps, the launcher is free, and the phone says Listening in the background with a Stop beside it.

    Under the hood

    Not a background-work permission: without an open audio lease there is nothing to keep alive. Releasing, revoking or losing the lease always ends it; reopening the plugin resumes it.

  • foundation

    The bus, and the identity it enforces

    Any APK may bind to the hub; installing one grants it nothing. A plugin is package + plugin id + signing certificate, and each capability is a separate grant, checked at the hub on every message.

    Under the hood

    Ten capabilities today — surfaces, ink_surface, microphone, stt, tts, camera, http_proxy, mediasync, assistant, wireless_debugging. A plugin does not have to be running: the hub binds it awake when traffic arrives, measured at ~1.6 s including cold start.

  • foundation

    One link, two paths, and the hub picks

    Control messages ride CXR-L; anything binary — images, photo sync — goes over SPP. A plugin never chooses, and never learns which path its bytes took.

    Under the hood

    1.1.1 flipped that order to route around a link that reported sends it had not delivered, and 1.1.2 flipped it back: SPP is one RFCOMM channel with one write lock, so a control message queued behind a photo chunk waits for the whole chunk. The real fix is an acknowledgement, not a different running order.

  • foundation

    Setup without a computer

    Seven steps on the phone, the glasses app pushed over the Rokid link, then a two-card self-arm on the glasses. No cable, no PC, no ADB at any point.

    Under the hood

    Wireless Debugging self-pairing with an app-private KADB TLS identity and a detached watchdog — the classic ADB key is never touched, so nothing on a PC is ever enrolled. Navigation reads the firmware's own localized labels, so it works in every language the ROM ships.

  • foundation

    The distribution loop, closed

    The SDK publishes to JitPack from sdk-v* tags, a manifest PR lands a plugin in the public registry, and the in-app Store verifies SHA-256 and signer before the install runs.

    Under the hood

    Provenance is checked in a fixed order — commit, tag, build, publish. Everything self-updates afterwards: phone from releases, glasses over CXR, plugins from the Store. Since 1.4.7 the tests of both hubs and every plugin run on each push, and a release that fails them is not published.

  • foundation

    All five display tiers, and the motion under them

    Ambient, pin, activity, notice, surface — plus a shared glyph set, plugin marks sent as bare geometry, and a status row along the bottom of every plugin surface.

    Under the hood

    Three duration tokens and two interpolators, nothing a plugin can dial. Native Views, not a WebView: the WebView spike rendered the same motion for ~1.2 cores, +88 MB PSS and 2.2 s to first paint, against 7.7 % CPU. The status row — phone charge, date, glasses charge — arrived in 1.4.8.

  • foundation

    The camera capability

    The glasses stream live H.264 and the plugin decodes on the phone, where ML Kit runs OCR and translation offline. No glasses-side plugin code exists.

    Under the hood

    A phone app cannot switch its own Wi-Fi on, which used to be a dead end. Now the roles invert — the phone hosts a LocalOnlyHotspot and the glasses join it — and the wire protocol, the decode, the overlays and freeze are unchanged.

  • 1.4.9

    Speech, in both directions

    stt takes words from your mouth, tts says them back with your phone's voice — through the glasses or your earbuds, never the phone's own speaker. How long it waits for you is now a setting.

    Under the hood

    Voice and speed are picked once, for everything that speaks. Opening the microphone silences speech, or the glasses would record their own voice into your dictation. Patience covers both the wait for the first word and the pause that ends a sentence; the Android recognizer only starts once you do.

  • foundation

    Waking a dark display, without owning it

    A notice worth it can pulse the display awake: at most once every five seconds across every plugin, never held on. No other tier may do it at all.

  • 1.4.1

    Ink Surface

    A plugin sends a strict subset of Rokid's .ink format; the phone compiles it and the glasses draw it as native Views. Charts, progress, tap actions, inline Lottie — no WebView, no JavaScript, no page-side network.

  • 1.4.2

    The phone as a remote for native glasses apps

    List and open the apps already on the glasses, move focus, type into their fields, and drag the system's own pointer like a trackpad — in the Rokid launcher and in any third-party app.

    Under the hood

    The four /core/* protocol families are hub-only and replay-safe; no plugin grant reaches them. Sensitive editors secure the phone window, and existing field contents never cross back to the phone.

  • 1.4.6

    Typed input for plugins

    A card can carry one text field. A keyboard bonded to the glasses works, and so does the phone's Keyboard & remote screen. Relay replies and Assistant notes came first. Built by ruruw.

  • 1.4.8

    The assist button, on your terms

    A plugin can replace the glasses assistant without owning the button: hand it back to Rokid's assistant and the plugin stays one pick away in the launcher. Plugins are told why they were opened.

Deferred

Designed, and parked on purpose until a concrete case asks for them.

  • Display policies and ownership epochs

    Foreground ownership, per-surface ordering, sequenced hides and image-decode invalidation already protect the normal handoff, and "newest notice wins" is a deliberate single-slot policy, not a missing queue.

    Per-plugin mute, demote and notices-only, and hub-stamped ownership epochs, exist on development branches. They come back against a reproduced scenario, not as a global display rework on principle.

  • Continuous speech

    Speech to text ships in short takes. Since 1.4.10 a microphone lease can outlive its surface, but that is not transcription that runs for minutes.

    The missing slice is speech held open with partial results streaming to the HUD, and captions that survive the surface underneath changing. Live captions and translation need it; it waits for the first consumer that does.

Next

Committed, not started. In this order.

  • A navigation plugin

    Google Maps and Citymapper already emit turn-by-turn as notifications. The plugin reads those and keeps the route as an activity, with notices for the moments that matter.

    A platform nav kind — maneuver glyph, distance, street, ETA — comes after those payloads have proven which fields are stable, not before.

  • Skills, and a keyboard for Assistant

    A hub-mediated registry so Assistant can pause music or ask Transit without binding another plugin or reimplementing it. Plus a phone-typed ask, for the places where talking to your glasses is not an option.

  • Native apps in the Nexus menu

    The phone already lists and opens the apps on the glasses. Phase two puts them behind the same triple-tap that lists plugins, with a back that lands where you started. Nexus does not port them and does not wrap them.

  • Maven Central

    JitPack builds the SDK from tags and is fine for early adopters, but it is not something a serious app should depend on. Central goes out once the AIDL surface is a promise rather than a snapshot.

  • A control-plane acknowledgement

    MediaSync already acks photo chunks. Glasses→phone CXR still reports success for frames the third-party client never sees, which is why outbound traffic prefers SPP. Another flip of running order is not the fix.

Never

Non-goals, stated once so nobody has to ask twice.

  • Native glasses apps are launched, never absorbed

    Porting them into Nexus would make the platform responsible for software it did not write. The menu starts them; that is the whole relationship.

  • Plugin code stays on the phone

    The glasses half of any capability lives in the hub. That is exactly what makes zero glasses-side deployment possible, and it is not negotiable.

  • No signature-only plugin permission

    It would be the strongest trust model available, and it would also make third-party plugins impossible — no external developer can be signed with our key.

  • One host, one platform, for now

    Hi Rokid Global and Android. Anything else is a promise we cannot currently keep on hardware we can test.

Eleven plugins, none of them built in

Every one is an ordinary phone APK installed from the in-app Store, pinned by hash and signer before it installs. The hub itself ships empty. These are real captures off the optic, uncropped.

Installation alone grants nothing. Surfaces, Ink, microphone, speech to text, text to speech, camera, photos, HTTP proxy, the assistant slot and wireless debugging are approved one by one, per plugin, keyed to its package, its plugin id, and its signing certificate — and checked at the hub on every message, not at install time.

Get started

Running it takes a phone and four steps. Writing for it takes one Gradle coordinate.

Run it

  1. Install the phone app. One APK from the releases page — it is the hub, the Store, and the setup guide.
  2. Follow the seven steps. The phone drives the pairing, turns the glasses' Wi-Fi on, and pushes the glasses app over the Rokid link.
  3. Say yes once, on the glasses. Enable accessibility when asked; Nexus bootstraps the rest of its glasses-side setup itself.
  4. Install a plugin from the Store. Everything keeps itself current afterwards.

No cable, no PC, no ADB at any point — including the privileged parts, which the glasses arm by themselves in two cards.

Write for it

  1. Depend on the SDK. :bus-client brings the service base class, the typed surfaces, the lifecycle callbacks and the design kit.
  2. Declare who you are. A headless service, a plugin id, a 24×24 mark in one green, and the capabilities you need — no launcher icon.
  3. Push a surface. Describe content, never pixels. Your process does not need to be running: the hub binds you awake.
  4. Publish, optionally. You host the APK, the registry indexes it, the Store pins your hash and your signer.
build.gradle.kts
repositories { maven("https://jitpack.io") }

dependencies {
    implementation(
        "com.github.Anezium.Rokid-Nexus:bus-client:sdk-v0.19.0"
    )
}
